About the data

Everything here comes from data the City publishes, plus the DCAS exam pages. No numbers are estimated, modelled or filled in. Where a field is missing it is left out rather than guessed at, which is why some pages are shorter than others.

What this cannot tell you

Whether you will be hired. Nothing published shows how far down a list an agency has reached, or where any individual sits. This site has no name search and no list number lookup, by choice.

How fast a list moves. The certification data records that a list was used, not how deep. Some titles are certified hundreds of times because every posting pulls a few names. Uniformed titles are certified a handful of times in large batches. A high count means the title is hired often, not that the list moves quickly.

What you would earn. A title's salary range is what it is authorized to pay across every assignment level. Where we show a median it is a different statistic, from a different source, describing people currently in the job.

Anything about a person. The City publishes candidate names, exact scores and credit flags in the active list dataset. This site never requests those columns, never caches them and never publishes them. That is enforced in the pipeline rather than promised in prose.

Known holes, stated plainly

A missing year. No exam has an application period between July 2024 and June 2025. The City never published that fiscal year's schedule as open data. The archive on this site starts after the gap rather than showing a partial record that would look complete.

The feed lags the City. When DCAS extends an application period, its own pages change first and the open data follows, sometimes weeks later. On one check the City's pages listed eight exams open and the data listed two. The pipeline reads both every day and lets the City's pages win on dates.

Truncated names. The title catalog cuts names at 30 characters, and about half of all rows hit that limit. Where an exam or a list spells a title out in full, that fuller spelling is what you see.

Recycled exam numbers. The City reuses exam numbers roughly every ten years. Any history attached to an exam here is filtered to the period after that list was established, so an old, unrelated exam's record is not credited to a current one.

Where it comes from

How often it updates

A scheduled job runs the pipeline once a day, rebuilds the files this site reads, and commits only when something actually changed.

Exam dates are also published as a calendar. Each exam page has a button that adds that one exam to your calendar, with a reminder before it closes. If you would rather have all of them, the whole feed is here, though for most people one exam at a time is the useful version.

The date on every page is the date the City's data says it is current as of, not the date this site last ran. Those are different, and the second one is the useless one: a build that runs perfectly every day on data that stopped updating in April is exactly the failure this site is built to make visible.
