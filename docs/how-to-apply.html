How this works

Most jobs with the City of New York are filled from an exam. Not an interview first, an exam. You take it, you get a score, the score puts you in a queue, and agencies hire from the top of that queue. It is a slow system and an unusually fair one, and almost nobody explains it to you before you need it.

The short version

Find an exam for a job you want. Apply during its application period, which is usually about a month long and is the only time you can apply. Take the exam. Get a score. Wait to be called.

Step by step

1. Find an exam
Exams are announced on a schedule the City publishes once a year, and most application periods open on the first Wednesday of a month. The front page shows what is open and what is coming. If nothing is open for the job you want, that is normal. Some titles run an exam every year, some every four or five years, some almost never.
2. Read the Notice of Examination
The Notice is the real document. It lists the requirements, the fee, what the test covers, and the dates. Nothing on this site replaces it. Where a Notice has been published we link straight to the City's PDF.
3. Apply through OASys
nyc.gov/examsforjobs is the City's application system. You need an account there. There is usually a fee, and it can be waived if you receive public assistance. Apply before the last day rather than on it. The system gets busy and the deadline does not move.
4. Take the exam
Most are multiple choice at a test centre. Some are physical, some are judged on your education and experience rather than a test sitting.
5. Get your score and your list number
Passing puts you on a civil service list, ranked by score. Your list number is your place in that queue. Ties are usually broken by when you filed. Veterans and some other groups can add points to a passing score, which changes where they land.
6. Wait to be certified
When an agency has a vacancy it asks for the top of the list, which is called a certification, and interviews from the names it gets. Getting certified is not the same as getting hired. Lists usually run four years and can be extended. Whether you are reached depends on your number, how many passed, and how much the City is hiring for that title.

Words the City uses

Open competitive
An exam anyone can take. You do not need to work for the City.
Promotion
An exam only current City employees in a related title can take.
Title
The job itself, as the civil service defines it, with a code, a salary range and a union. Every title has a page here.
Application period
The window when applications are accepted. Miss it and you wait for the next exam.
Civil service list
Everyone who passed a given exam, in score order.
Certification
An agency pulling names off a list to fill a vacancy.
Provisional
Someone doing a civil service job without having come off a list, usually because no list exists yet. It is meant to be temporary.

Two honest warnings

It is slow. Months between applying and sitting the exam, months between the exam and the list, and possibly years on the list. People who get City jobs this way generally applied and then got on with their lives.

Dates move. DCAS extends application periods fairly often, and the open data feed this site reads can lag the City's own pages by weeks. We reconcile against those pages daily and let them win, but before you rely on a date, check nyc.gov.

This is not a City website. The City's own glossary is at nyc.gov, and where it has a word for something, we use the same word.
