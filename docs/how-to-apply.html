How to apply

DCAS, the City agency that runs this system, says more than 80% of City jobs are filled through a civil service exam. You apply during a set window, take the exam, get a score, and the score places you on a list for that title. What happens next depends a great deal on which job you are after, and the difference is worth knowing before you start.

Two ways this actually plays out

For many entry-level and uniformed titles, the list is the hiring queue. Police Officer, Sanitation Worker, Emergency Medical Specialist. You pass, you are ranked, and agencies work down the list as they hire. Your number is close to the whole story, and waiting is most of the process.

For many professional and managerial titles, the list is a door rather than a queue. Being reachable on the list for a title like Administrative Staff Analyst is what makes you eligible for the postings that require it. You still apply, still interview, still compete on your experience. The exam is not the thing that gets you the job, it is the thing that lets you be considered for a whole class of jobs you otherwise cannot hold permanently.

One rule touches both. New York State's one in three rule lets an agency pick any of the top three scorers on a Certified List, not only the single highest name. For high-volume hiring that rarely changes who gets reached, since the agency works through the list fast enough that score still decides most of it. For a single opening it is the reason two similarly ranked candidates can both get an interview, and the interview decides from there.

Both are the same system seen from different places in it. Which one you are in depends on the title, so it is worth asking people already doing the job rather than assuming.

The short version

Find an exam for a title you want. Apply during its application period, which usually runs around three weeks and is the only time you can apply. Take the exam. Get a score. What follows is either a wait to be called or a set of doors that open, depending on the title.

Step by step

1. Find an exam
Exams are announced on a schedule the City publishes once a year, and most application periods open on the first Wednesday of a month. The front page shows what is open and what is coming. If nothing is open for the job you want, that is normal. Some titles run an exam every year, some every four or five years, some almost never.
2. Read the Notice of Examination
The Notice is the real document. It lists the minimum qualifications, the fee, what the test covers, and the dates. Nothing on this site replaces it. Where a Notice has been published we link straight to the City's PDF.
3. Apply through OASys
OASys, the City's Online Application System, is at nyc.gov/examsforjobs. You need an account there. DCAS says most exams can be taken online or at a testing center in any borough, and there is usually a fee, which can be waived if you receive public assistance. Apply before the last day rather than on it. The system gets busy and the deadline does not move.
4. Take the exam
Most are multiple choice. Some are practical or physical tests of a specific skill, and some, DCAS calls these Education and Experience exams, are judged on your background rather than a test sitting.
5. Get your score and your list number
DCAS says results typically take six to nine months to process. When they are ready your score posts to your OASys dashboard and DCAS emails it to you, along with the list of everyone who passed, ranked by score. That list is your civil service list, and your list number is your place on it. Ties are usually broken by when you filed. Veterans and some other groups can add points to a passing score, which changes where they land.
6. Be reachable, then be certified
When an agency has a vacancy, DCAS sends it a Certified List: your list, or the top part of it. Under the one in three rule the agency can interview and choose any of the top three scorers it receives, not only the highest name, so being certified is not the same as being hired. Lists usually run four years and can be extended. How much your number matters varies: for some titles it is nearly the whole decision, for others being reachable is the point and the hiring is a normal competitive process from there.

Words the City uses

Open competitive
Anyone meeting the minimum qualifications in the Notice can take it. You do not need to work for the City.
Promotion
Open only to City employees who already hold permanent Civil Service status in a related title, meaning they were appointed from an earlier exam and finished their probationary period.
Title
The job itself, as the civil service defines it, with a code, a salary range and a union. Every title has a page here.
Application period
The window when applications are accepted. Miss it and you wait for the next exam. Some older Notices call this the filing period. Same thing.
Civil service list
Everyone who passed a given exam, in score order.
Certified list
Your list, or the top part of it, once DCAS sends it to an agency to hire from.
Certification
An agency pulling names off a list to fill a vacancy.
One in three rule
The New York State law letting an agency choose any of the top three scorers it receives on a certified list, not only the highest one.
Provisional
Someone doing a civil service job without having come off a list, usually because no list exists yet. It is meant to be temporary.

Two honest warnings

It is slow. Months between applying and sitting the exam, months between the exam and the list, and possibly years on the list. People who get City jobs this way generally applied and then got on with their lives.

Dates move. DCAS extends application periods fairly often, and the open data feed this site reads can lag the City's own pages by weeks. We reconcile against those pages daily and let them win, but before you rely on a date, check nyc.gov.
